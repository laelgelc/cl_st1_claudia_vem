## Proposed interpretation of f3 (positive)

**Recommended label: Institutional coordination and inclusive professional formation through virtual exchange**

This pole encodes discourse that presents international collaboration as an **institutionally organised, actively supported route to students’ global professional development**. It combines accounts of partnership-building and project implementation with advocacy for wider participation, intercultural learning, and the formation of globally capable young professionals.

The interpretation is broader than any one project topic: the excerpts connect international offices, institutional leaders, teachers, and student teams within a shared discourse of making international learning work and extending its benefits.

## 1. What the lexical loadings contribute

The loadings suggest several overlapping components rather than a single, tightly bounded subject field.

- **Institutional infrastructure and communication:** *escritório* (.88), *e-mail* (.74), and *gerenciar* (.41) support an organisational reading. In the excerpts, *escritório* identifies international or global-learning offices and their directors. *E-mail* marks the channel through which an institutional interview was obtained; it should not, by itself, be treated as evidence about students’ collaborative communication.
- **Dated reporting of project activity:** *fevereiro* (.86) anchors accounts of awards, partnership meetings, and the initiation of student interactions. Its strong loading indicates that time-specific reporting contributes substantially to the pole, although February itself is not a discourse theme.
- **Participation, reach, and beneficiaries:** *menos* (.88), *inclusão* (.50), *jovem* (.49), *tecnólogo* (.39), and *todo* (.31) collectively support a discourse of participation and educational benefit. Their functions are context-dependent: *menos* occurs in minimum-participation formulations such as “pelo menos uma vez” and in the affordability claim “menos custosa”; *todo* helps express collective involvement or broad coverage.
- **Effort and project content:** *energia* (.69) has two distinct meanings in the samples: teachers’ energy and sustained presence, and energy as a collaborative research topic. Both must be retained; the loading cannot be reduced to motivational language alone.
- **A less securely interpretable contribution:** *revelar* (.34) lacks direct illustration in the supplied excerpts, so it should carry limited interpretive specificity.

Thus, the lexical evidence supports institutional organisation, participation, and formation, but also shows that **reporting conventions and particular project topics help constitute the factor**.

## 2. What the examples contribute

The excerpts supply the discourse relationships that the loading list alone cannot establish.

**Institutional partnership-building and leadership** are prominent in the Durban international-office interview, the Viseu partnership report, and the Florida International University collaboration. These texts identify responsible actors, describe coordination, and present collaboration as a planned institutional undertaking.

**Practical facilitation and sustained engagement** are especially clear in “Presença e energia.” Successful collaboration requires aligned teachers, group leadership, linguistic support, monitoring, and attention to interpersonal skills. Here, the loading words *menos*, *todo*, and *energia* occur within advice about keeping participants involved. The resilience report similarly describes adjustments that allow projects to continue despite disruption.

**Inclusive access and global professional formation** connect these operational accounts to their educational rationale. The edition 08 interview links *menos* to lower-cost participation and *jovem* to cultural intelligence, innovation, and professional preparation. Edition 01 repeatedly uses *tecnólogo* in claims about international professionalisation and linguistic, intercultural, and teamwork competencies. The Durban interview explicitly advocates inclusion and diversity and proposes participation by all students at least once.

These examples therefore support an **enabling and legitimating discourse**: they explain how international collaboration is organised, why it deserves institutional support, and what students gain from participating.
